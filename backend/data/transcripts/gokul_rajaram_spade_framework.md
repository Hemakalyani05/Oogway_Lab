# Episode: Gokul Rajaram on the SPADE Framework for High-Stakes Decision Making
**Guest:** Gokul Rajaram (Board Member at Coinbase, Pinterest, ex-DoorDash, Square, Google)
**Host:** Lenny Rachitsky
**Date:** 2023-01-24
**URL:** https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/gokul-rajaram-spade-framework

---

[00:01:10] Lenny Rachitsky: Gokul, you are known in Silicon Valley as the "godfather of product frameworks." One framework you developed at Square that is used everywhere is the SPADE framework for high-stakes decision making. How does SPADE work?

[00:02:50] Gokul Rajaram: Thanks Lenny. Most companies make decisions by consensus, which leads to slow, watered-down compromise, or they make decisions by top-down dictatorship without consulting stakeholders. SPADE is a five-step framework designed to make fast, high-quality, documented decisions:

1. **S = Setting:** Define the context, the problem statement, the timeline for the decision, and what is at stake.
2. **P = People:** Explicitly define the roles. There must be exactly ONE **Responsible Person** (the Decider). Then define the **Consulted** (people with domain expertise whose input is required), the **Approver** (usually the executive sponsor who can veto, but rarely does), and the **Informed** (who need to know the outcome).
3. **A = Alternatives:** You must brainstorm at least 3 distinct, viable alternatives—not fake strawmen. Each alternative must list pros, cons, costs, and risks.
4. **D = Decide:** The single Decider makes the call. They write down the decision and why they chose it over the alternatives.
5. **E = Explain:** The Decider holds a meeting or sends a written memo explaining the decision to the Consulted and Informed stakeholders.

[00:07:30] Gokul Rajaram: The most important rule in SPADE is the **"Disagree and Commit"** principle. Stakeholders have the right and obligation to disagree vehemently during the 'Consult' phase, but once the Decider executes step D and explains step E, everyone must rally behind the decision 100%.

[00:10:15] Lenny Rachitsky: When should a team use SPADE versus a quick meeting?

[00:11:40] Gokul Rajaram: Use SPADE for Type 1 (irreversible) decisions or decisions that affect multiple departments—like pricing changes, architectural platform shifts, or entering a new market. For Type 2 (reversible) decisions, just move fast and experiment.
