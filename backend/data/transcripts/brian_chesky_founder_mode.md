# Episode: Brian Chesky on Founder Mode & Reimagining Product Management at Airbnb
**Guest:** Brian Chesky (Co-founder & CEO, Airbnb)
**Host:** Lenny Rachitsky
**Date:** 2024-09-08
**URL:** https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/brian-chesky-founder-mode

---

[00:01:15] Lenny Rachitsky: Welcome to the podcast. Today I have Brian Chesky, co-founder and CEO of Airbnb. Brian, your recent talk at Y Combinator about "Founder Mode" sparked a massive global conversation about how tech companies should actually be run. What did you mean by Founder Mode?

[00:02:30] Brian Chesky: Thanks Lenny. For years, founders were told by traditional advisors and business schools that as a company scales, you have to switch to "Manager Mode." Manager mode means you hire professional managers, delegate everything, tell them what outcome you want, and step back. You become an orchestrator who only looks at dashboards.

[00:04:10] Brian Chesky: What we learned the hard way—especially during the pandemic when our revenue dropped 80% in eight weeks—is that Manager Mode breaks companies. When you delegate everything to autonomous business units, you end up with 50 sub-companies, 50 roadmaps, endless political fiefdoms, and nobody actually looking at the integrated product experience.

[00:06:45] Brian Chesky: Founder Mode is the opposite. It is not micromanagement; it is deep, obsessive involvement in the details that matter. As a founder or product leader, you must know the details of the product. Steve Jobs did not manage Apple through delegation spreadsheets; he reviewed every product weekly. We got rid of the traditional divisional product management structure at Airbnb and moved to a single integrated roadmap.

[00:09:20] Lenny Rachitsky: You made headlines when you said you "got rid of traditional product management." What did Airbnb replace it with?

[00:10:45] Brian Chesky: We didn't eliminate the function; we elevated and merged it. We combined Product Management with Product Marketing into a single role: Product Marketing Managers (PMMs). Why? Because in many tech companies, product managers had become project coordinators writing Jira tickets and passing messages between engineers and designers. They were disconnected from the customer and disconnected from storytelling.

[00:13:10] Brian Chesky: In our model, if you build a product, you have to be able to explain and sell the product. We run two massive synchronized product releases every year—a Summer Release and a Winter Release. Everything is coordinated. Every Sunday night, I personally review the roadmap and major feature mockups with our design and engineering leads.

[00:16:00] Lenny Rachitsky: What advice do you have for product managers operating under this model?

[00:17:30] Brian Chesky: Be a product person, not a bureaucrat. Great product managers have extreme taste, deep empathy for users, and the ability to think in systems rather than isolated feature silos. Don't hide behind A/B tests to make decisions you should make with conviction and customer understanding.
