[00:01:00] Lenny Rachitsky: After interviewing founders and growth leaders from over 100 top tech companies—including Figma, Notion, Linear, Superhuman, Substack, and Stripe—I analyzed the exact launch strategies that created category-defining products.

[00:02:30] Lenny Rachitsky: Here are the 7 core launch archetypes used by the best companies:
1. **The Exclusive Waitlist & Scarcity Loop:** Used by Superhuman, Robinhood, and Clubhouse. Limit access, make onboarding feel bespoke, and leverage social status.
2. **The Community-First Seeding Launch:** Used by Notion and Figma. Seed the beta in highly engaged Discord/Slack communities, Reddit, and Twitter designer circles. Let power users create public templates and tutorials before public launch.
3. **The High-Production Apple-Style Synchronized Event:** Used by Airbnb and Linear. Rather than continuous random feature drops, bundle 10-20 improvements into a major seasonal launch (e.g., Airbnb Summer Release) to capture mainstream press attention.
4. **The Direct Founder Outreach / Do Things That Don't Scale:** Stripe and Airbnb in early days. Founders personally onboarded early users, installed code on their laptops, and listened to feedback.
5. **The Side Project & Free Tool Launch:** HubSpot's Website Grader or Buffer's free social tools. Build a lightweight standalone tool that solves an adjacent problem and drives organic pipeline.
6. **The Micro-Influencer & Thought Leadership Blitz:** Seed the product with 50 respected niche influencers who post organic testimonials simultaneously.
7. **The Product Hunt & Hacker News Grassroots Blitz:** Authentic, transparent stories directly from makers on community launchboards.
