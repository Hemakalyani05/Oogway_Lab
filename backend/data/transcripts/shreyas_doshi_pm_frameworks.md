# Episode: Shreyas Doshi on Good PM vs Great PM & The LNO Framework
**Guest:** Shreyas Doshi (ex-Stripe, Twitter, Google Product Leader & Executive Coach)
**Host:** Lenny Rachitsky
**Date:** 2023-04-18
**URL:** https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/shreyas-doshi-pm-frameworks

[00:01:05] Lenny Rachitsky: Shreyas, you've trained thousands of product managers and leaders. One of your most influential mental models is the LNO Framework for task and time management. Can you break that down?

[00:02:40] Shreyas Doshi: Absolutely, Lenny. Most PMs feel permanently overwhelmed because they treat all tasks as equally deserving of perfection. The LNO Framework categorizes work into three distinct buckets:
1. **L = Leverage Tasks:** High-leverage work where the difference between good and exceptional output is 10x to 100x impact. Examples: product strategy, critical roadmap prioritization, customer discovery for next year's core product line, hiring top talent. You should invest 100% of your energy and obsess over perfection here.
2. **N = Neutral Tasks:** Work where doing a good job is necessary, but doing a 10x job yields zero additional value. Examples: weekly status updates, regular sprint planning, basic bug triage. You should do these at a "good enough" standard (80% quality) with minimum time.
3. **O = Overhead Tasks:** Administrative chores with low upside and minor downside. Examples: filling out corporate surveys, routine email acknowledgments, expense reports. You should execute these with speed, delegate them, or batch them at the end of the day.

[00:06:50] Shreyas Doshi: If you spend all day perfecting Neutral and Overhead tasks, you have no creative capacity left for Leverage tasks. Great PMs protect their calendar for Leverage work.

[00:09:15] Lenny Rachitsky: What separates a Good Product Manager from a Great Product Manager?

[00:11:00] Shreyas Doshi: Good PMs focus on execution and delivery—they ship on time and manage stakeholders well. Great PMs focus on **outcomes, high agency, and clarity of thought**. 
A Great PM asks: *"Even if this ships perfectly, does it change the trajectory of our company or solve a true user pain?"*
Great PMs have high agency: when a project is blocked by external dependencies, they find creative ways around the wall rather than citing reasons why it's impossible.
