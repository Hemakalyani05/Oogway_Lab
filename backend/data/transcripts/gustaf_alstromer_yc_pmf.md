# Episode: Gustaf Alströmer on How Y Combinator Evaluates Product-Market Fit (PMF)
**Guest:** Gustaf Alströmer (Group Partner at Y Combinator, ex-Head of Growth at Airbnb)
**Host:** Lenny Rachitsky
**Date:** 2023-05-30
**URL:** https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/gustaf-alstromer-yc-pmf

---

[00:01:15] Lenny Rachitsky: Gustaf, as a YC Partner, you see hundreds of early-stage startups every batch. How does Y Combinator objectively evaluate whether a company has true Product-Market Fit (PMF)?

[00:02:50] Gustaf Alströmer: Thanks Lenny. At YC, we define Product-Market Fit with quantitative metrics rather than feelings or press coverage. We look at four primary benchmarks:

1. **Cohort Retention Plateau:** The retention curve must flatten. For consumer apps, 20-30% retention at Day 30 is good; 40%+ is world-class. For B2B SaaS, monthly logo retention should stabilize above 90%, and net revenue retention (NRR) should exceed 110-120%.
2. **Organic / Word-of-Mouth Growth Velocity:** If more than 50% of your new users come from direct search, unpaid word-of-mouth, or peer referrals, customers love the product enough to talk about it without being incentivized.
3. **The 40% "Very Disappointed" Sean Ellis Benchmark:** If you survey active users and ask *"How would you feel if you could no longer use this product?"*, at least 40% must answer "Very disappointed."
4. **Revenue Pull vs. Push:** In true PMF, the market is pulling the product out of the company. Founders are overwhelmed by inbound support tickets, feature requests, and customers willing to pay even when the product is buggy.

[00:08:30] Lenny Rachitsky: What is the most common mistake founders make before finding PMF?

[00:10:00] Gustaf Alströmer: Scaling marketing prematurely. When founders don't have PMF, they hire salespeople or spend on Facebook/Google ads. They confuse temporary top-of-funnel spikes with real product-market fit. If retention is broken, growth will quickly stall.
